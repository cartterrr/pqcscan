About_□x

Why bother now?

A big enough quantum computer breaks RSA and elliptic-curve crypto. Nobody has one yet, but encrypted traffic recorded today can be decrypted the day someone does. That is why key exchange comes first in the plan and signatures second.

NIST finished the replacements in 2024: ML-KEM (FIPS 203) for key exchange, ML-DSA (FIPS 204) and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) for signatures. Every migration roadmap starts the same way: find out where the old crypto is. That is the job of this tool.

What the scan can't see

Rules match library calls, not data flow, so each finding needs a person to look at it. Compiled binaries, containers, HSMs, cloud KMS settings and third-party services are out of reach. An empty report does not mean the system is quantum-safe.

Same engine as the command line: pqcscan scan, pqcscan tls, pqcscan ui.
